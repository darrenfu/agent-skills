# Negentropy Lens

Use entropy (accumulating disorder or maintenance burden), negentropy (deliberate organization or compounding capability), and tacit knowledge (unwritten operational understanding) as an optional organizational metaphor. These labels are not a physical model or a substitute for engineering evidence.

## Apply the lens

1. Identify the relevant system boundary and requested decision. Reuse known context instead of requiring a new discovery questionnaire.
2. Trace important components, information flows, and ownership. Look for accumulating workarounds, fragile interfaces, and undocumented dependencies alongside reuse, learning, and improved reliability.
3. Consider tacit knowledge when it affects the decision: what differs between the documented process and actual operation, and what depends on a particular person? Ask the user only about material gaps that cannot be resolved from available evidence.
4. Compare options on capability, maintenance, resilience, knowledge transfer, reversibility, and cost. A system can have improving, deteriorating, stable, mixed, or unknown aspects. Inaction can be a rational choice; monitoring and routine maintenance can provide lasting value.
5. State a concrete recommendation, tradeoffs, and uncertainty. Avoid treating “growth” as inherently preferable to dependable operation or classifying every component into a forced binary.

Match the requested depth. For a quick decision, a few sentences may be sufficient. For an organizational or architecture analysis, map the relevant flows and support claims with observations. Introduce terminology only when it helps the audience, and do not inject this lens into unrelated decisions.

For the framework's conceptual background, read [origin-essay.md](references/origin-essay.md) as an opinion source, not empirical proof of universal system behavior.
